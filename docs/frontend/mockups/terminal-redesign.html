How Ground-Glass Light Tables Are Made

Results

A glassblower explains how ground glass gets its even, directionless diffusion — sandblasting first, then a fine abrasive slurry rubbed by hand — and why machine-ground panels scatter light unevenly at the edges. Three grit stages are shown: 80, 220, and 600, each changing the falloff of a projected grid pattern.

Closing segment covers annealing: panels held at 545°C for two hours, then cooled slowly over eight to avoid internal stress that would crack the glass under a hot projector lamp.

Even light isn't the glass being clear — it's the glass being evenly unclear.

A short walkthrough of how optical ground glass is finished by hand: two grit passes, a slurry rub, then a slow anneal so the panel won't crack under lamp heat. Ends with a side-by-side comparing machine-ground and hand-finished diffusion under a projected test grid.
